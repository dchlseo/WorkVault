# Rental-car use by international visitors to Korea — 2025 외래관광객조사

A Korean counterpart to the Japan FF-Data rental-car analysis, limited to what the
2025 International Visitor Survey (외래관광객조사, n = 16,110, weighted to 15.9 M visitors) can support.

To re-run: `python _build/build_notebook.py`, or open the notebook and run all cells.
Requires pandas, numpy, matplotlib, geopandas and openpyxl. The regression is written in numpy, so

## How this differs from the Japan FF-Data analysis

The Korean survey has **no trip legs, no per-movement transport mode and no entry/exit airport**.
So:

- **Not possible:** rental-car *share of movements* (mode share), access / within-region / between-region
  leg splits, entry-airport analyses and origin–destination flows (FF-Data Figs 2, 10–12).
- **Possible, at the respondent level:** whether a visitor used a rental car at any point on the trip, and
  how that varies by nationality, traveller characteristics and the provinces they visited.

**Definition of a rental-car user (primary):** rental-car spending > 0 (`대여서1인대체`) **or** a rental
car booked in advance (`Q5_1a03`). The advance-booking question was asked only of independent and Air-tel
travellers. Spending-only, booking-only, both, and fuel-spending definitions are reported as sensitivity checks.

**"Where":** the survey records only which provinces were visited and the days spent in each, not where the
car was driven. Two measures are used:

1. **Rental-user share of visitor-days.** A user's days in *every* province they visited count as rental-user
   days. This is an upper bound on in-province driving.
2. **Visitors who went to a single province.** This gives clean attribution, but only Jeju, Seoul, Busan,
   Gyeonggi and Incheon have enough respondents.

Estimates are weighted with `weight`. The 95% CIs are Wilson intervals on the Kish effective sample size.
The design strata and sampling clusters aren't released, so the CIs are approximate.

## Key results

### 1. About 1 in 25 visitors used a rental car (4.2%)
- Primary definition: **4.2%** of visitors (95% CI 3.8–4.6%), or about **670,000** visitors in 2025
  (730 respondents).
- By definition: spending only 2.5%, advance booking only 2.9%, both 1.1%. The two signals overlap less than
  expected (only 231 respondents report both), so the definition matters. See `01_user_share_by_definition`.
- Rental cars account for **5.8% of visitors' domestic transport spending** (6.8% including fuel). Bus and
  taxi account for 54% and rail for 33%. This is a spending share, not a mode share. Transport bought as
  part of a package tour isn't itemised.

### 2. Where: Jeju is the only province where rental cars are a mass-market mode
- **Jeju:** rental-car users account for **20% of visitor-days**. 18.5% of Jeju visitors used a rental
  car, and 15.7% of visitors who went **only** to Jeju did (n = 693). That is about **4× the national
  rate** (log2 = +2.0).
- **Everywhere else:** 0–8% of visitor-days. Chungnam (8%), Gyeongbuk (7%), Gangwon (6%) and Gyeongnam (6%)
  are above the national figure (4.9%). The capital area is below it: Seoul 4%, Incheon 3%, Gyeonggi 3%.
  Most non-capital provinces have fewer than 25 rental-user respondents, so their shares are imprecise.
- **Volume** still follows visitor volume: Seoul has the most rental-user visitor-days (2.7 M), ahead of
  Jeju (1.4 M) and Busan (0.4 M). This is partly an artefact of the upper-bound attribution, because most
  Jeju users also pass through Seoul.
- **Caution:** 225 of the 730 users visited *only* Seoul, which is 2.6% of Seoul-only visitors. Some of
  this is probably chauffeured cars or chartered vans recorded as "rental car", so the rental-car items
  likely mix self-drive and hired-with-driver vehicles.

### 3. Segments by nationality: volume comes from China and Taiwan; propensity is highest for Singapore
- **Share of all rental-car users:** China 31%, Taiwan 16%, USA 10%, Singapore 9%, Hong Kong 6%.
  Japan provides 21% of visitors but only 4% of users.
- **User share:** Singapore **16.6%**, Hong Kong 7.3%, Taiwan 6.4%, Malaysia 6.0%, USA 5.3%,
  China 4.5%. Lowest: Japan 0.8%, Middle East 0.9%, Vietnam 1.1%, Mongolia 0.2%.
- **Same-itinerary propensity index** (observed users ÷ users expected from Jeju visit × region pattern ×
  travel arrangement × length of stay): Singapore 2.5×, Taiwan 2.4×, Indonesia, Malaysia, Hong Kong and
  Philippines 1.7–1.9×; China 0.7×, Japan 0.4×, Middle East 0.2×. These differences are not explained
  by itinerary.
- **Within Jeju:** rental-car use among Jeju visitors ranges from **10% (China, n = 485)** to **72%
  (Singapore)** and **58% (Hong Kong)**. Taiwan is 28%, USA 25%. Outside Jeju, every nationality is
  below 8%.
- **Composition by province:** China (34%), Taiwan (18%) and Singapore (14%) make up most rental-user days
  in Jeju. Only Seoul and Jeju have enough users to break down reliably; other province columns are flagged.

### 4. Traveller characteristics
| Factor | Pattern (user share) |
|---|---|
| Travel arrangement | Independent 5.1% ≫ Air-tel 1.2% ≫ group tour 0.2% |
| Companions | With children **8.9%**, partner 5.7%, friends 3.5%, alone 2.3% |
| Length of stay | Peaks at 8–30 days (about 6.5%); 1–3 days 1.7%; 31+ days 1.9% |
| Provinces visited | 1 province 3.8% → 4+ provinces 8.6% |
| Region pattern | Capital area only 2.6% vs any non-capital province 7.3–7.8% |
| Age | Flat: 3–5% at every age, lowest under 20 and 60+ |
| Gender, quarter | No meaningful difference |
| Purpose | Leisure 4.6%; education 1.4% |
| Booking lead time | 6.8% for trips booked 3–6 months ahead vs 3–4% otherwise |

**Adjusted model** (weighted logistic regression; reference China / 20–29 / independent / capital area only
/ alone). Visiting Jeju is by far the strongest factor (**OR 10.2**). Next come nationality (Singapore 4.9,
Taiwan 3.9, Malaysia 3.3, Indonesia 3.3, Hong Kong 2.7, USA 2.1; Japan 0.4, Middle East 0.3), group tour
(OR 0.02), and travelling with children (2.4) or a partner (1.7). Age, gender, purpose and length of stay
are not significant once the other factors are controlled. Repeat visitors (4+) are modestly higher (1.4).

### 5. Regions reached (association only)
Users visit slightly more provinces than non-users (1.52 vs 1.34 on average; among Jeju visitors 1.62 vs 1.41).
They are **not** more likely to reach a non-capital mainland province (23% vs 24%). Unlike the Japan
result for non-hub entrants, there is no sign here that rental cars widen regional dispersion.

### 6. Spending and satisfaction
- Median rental spend among spenders is **about USD 100 per person** (weighted mean USD 150).
- Rental car plus fuel as a share of each nationality's domestic transport spending: Singapore 20%, then
  Taiwan, Malaysia, Hong Kong, Philippines and Indonesia at about 10%.
- Among independent and Air-tel travellers, users rate satisfaction slightly *lower* than non-users on
  every item. The largest gap is for transport (4.40 vs 4.54) and wayfinding (4.28 vs 4.38). The gaps
  are small but outside the CIs.

## Limitations
- **Respondent-level indicator only.** There is no frequency or duration of car use and no in-province
  attribution, and the province shares are upper bounds.
- **Measurement.** The rental-car spending and advance-booking signals disagree for about two-thirds of
  users, and some "rental" is likely hired cars with a driver. Self-drive use outside Jeju is probably
  overstated.
- **Small cells.** Outside Jeju and the capital area, provinces have 1–55 rental-user respondents. Many
  nationality × province and nationality × age cells are suppressed or flagged.
- **Coverage.** Group-tour travellers weren't asked the advance-booking question (they almost never rent).
  The survey is collected on departure, but the airport isn't released, so KAC airport-level analysis
  needs another source.
